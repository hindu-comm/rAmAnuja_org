SAtvika AhamkAram 4.09: "HERE COMES THE BENEVOLENT TORNADO!"
=============================================================viswa Anugraha 
mAtharam vyathishjath svarga ApavarghaAm sudhA -
sadhreechim ithi VenkaTEswara kavir bhakthyA dhayAm asthutha /
padhyAnAm iha yadh vidhEya bhagavath sankalpa kalpa dhrumAth
jhanjhA mArutha dhootha chootha nayatha: sAmpAthikO ayam krama: //
(DayA Sathakam SlOkam 107)

Meaning
"DayA DEvi is like the sweetest nectar (sudhA sadriseem) 

She is the one dearest to all her children in the Universe whomshe alwyas 
loves and blesses uncondionally. (viswa anugraha mAtharam)

Like a mother who holds several gifts to present to her children according to 
their taste and predilections, DayA Devi holds Svargam, Moksham and many 
other fruits for distribution according to our wishes. 
(vyathishjath svarga ApavarghaAm)

TiruvEnkaTamuDaiyAn's "Sankalpam" is like the divine "Kalpaka vriksham" whose 
branches are ever ready to yield countless ripe fruits.
(yadh vidhEya bhagavath sankalpa kalpa dhrumAth)

When the gales of "KaruNa" of DayA Devi lashes through, the fruits of the 
tree of "Bhagavad Sankalpam" fall (jhanjhA mArutha dhootha chootha nayatha:) 
not one by one but in clusters- all at the same time. 
(sAmpAthikO ayam krama:)

It is the compassion filled Sankalpam of Lord Srinivasa that has blossomed 
into this StOtram through ME by dint of my unequalled devotion to His 
compassion. (VenkaTEswara kavir bhakthyA dhayAm asthutha)

If you wish to enjoy the abundant crop of fruits- 
Come. 
Read this. 
You will get whatever you want!"

Comment:
Can a tornado ever be benevolent?
"Yes" says SwAmi.

When DayA DEvi's blessings showers, it pours and pours and pours-
Not singly, but in never ending clusters and bunches of benefits.

SwAmi commences this StOtra with the words "PrapadhyE" which means 
"I surrender"
He concludes it with the word "Kshamayathi" meaning "forgives"

The message is that the Lord out of His compassion non-pareil, excuses the 
faults of SaraNAgathas and showers benevolence abundantly like the trees 
yielding fruits as described above.
